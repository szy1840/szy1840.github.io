---
title: "Input Is the Hard Part Now"
lang: en
ref: input-output
---

Output has never been easier. Even people with no CS background can vibe-code their way through a project — and it's not just software; most white-collar work is trending the same direction. The floor for output is falling fast.

Which means input is both more important and harder to protect. You have to consciously believe in its value and deliberately carve out space for it.

Against that backdrop, I've become skeptical of the "open many sub-agents" workflow. At its core, that's just trying to raise output throughput. Context-switching has a fixed cost — keeping multiple agent sessions in your head is draining. More importantly, I think the right use of the time while an agent is working is to do input: review what it just changed, audit the architecture it's proposing, learn the background behind a particular implementation choice, identify the decision nodes that actually need a human. Long term, that compounds more than raw output throughput.

My current workflow: one session owns impl, one session owns deep dive.
